# Hicasso invariant and capability ledger

One page to check a change against: every invariant the product commits to, every capability and the rent it pays, and the provisional facade as the specification currently spells it.

Nothing here is new. Every row is transcribed from the [product specification](specification.md), the [design laws](lanes/design-laws.md), the [public-language lane](lanes/ergonomics-api.md), or the [decision brief](decision-brief.md), and cites its owner. **Where a row and its owner disagree the owner governs and the row is the defect** — fix the row; questions are not settled here.

Cite key: `§n` is a [product specification](specification.md) section; a capitalised word plus a number, such as *React 5*, is that numbered law in the named section of the [design laws](lanes/design-laws.md).

### The callback and frame-incarnation rule (rf2-hic-013)

**The rule.** A frame's public keyword id names an address, not an object. Destroying a frame and creating another under the same id produces a new incarnation, distinct by identity, and every operation minted against the predecessor — a captured bundle, a lowered callback, a retained handle — belongs to the predecessor for the rest of its life. Fired after the successor seats, such an operation writes nothing and refuses with `:rf.error/frame-destroyed`. **Where a delayed operation lands is fixed when it is minted, never when it is invoked**: an operation that resolves its target at call time resolves an address whose occupant has changed, which is a revival rather than a dispatch.

**What is witnessed.** `implementation/hicasso/test/re_frame/hicasso/reincarnation_routing_cljs_test.cljs` and its `_cells_` companion. The arm's frame-locked bundle obeys the rule — `:dispatch`, `:dispatch-sync` and `:subscribe` each refuse across the transition and fan exactly one always-on record, while an *unpinned* capture of the same shape does write the successor, so the refusal is the incarnation pin's doing and not an unresolved miss. On the committed side the teardown drops the cell's reaction synchronously and rebuilds it at the microtask checkpoint, and that deferred callback routes to the live incarnation when one exists and disposes exactly — zero cells, references and edges — when none does, which is [I5](#1-invariants)'s exact-teardown clause and [React 2](lanes/design-laws.md#react-and-ownership)'s *disconnect releases it* measured at the frame boundary.

**Two things the transition is invisible to, which is why the narrowing is worth recording.** `commit-basis` ties across a same-id reincarnation — its frame term restarts and neither of the other two is a frame fact — so the number React re-reads through `useSyncExternalStore` does not move when the successor seats, and no re-render is scheduled. Meanwhile the read path is address-directed on the public id, so a body re-run inside that window already paints the successor's value. Rendered markup is therefore a green instrument throughout, in both directions, and only the incarnation token, the memo's identity, the refusal record and the snapshot number distinguish *repaired* from *never broken*.

**Two items this freeze recorded rather than settled, both since settled elsewhere.** First, a **defect, since repaired by ruling** (rf2-x874): the ambient dispatch a lowered callback captured closed over the frame keyword and resolved its bundle when the callback fired, so a retained callback reached whichever incarnation the memo happened to describe at that instant — refused if some earlier dispatch had left the predecessor's bundle memoised, and a **silent write into the successor** if not. Exact eviction on destruction does not fix that and makes it universal; pinning the incarnation into the closure at mint time was measured to fix both branches, and that is what landed. `impl/frames.cljs` now keeps **one row per public id**, carrying the incarnation the row was minted under together with the `capture-frame` bundle pinned to it and the ambient dispatch closure over that same bundle, so the three can never describe different incarnations. A lookup compares the row's token against the incarnation live at that instant and a mismatch rebuilds the row, which is why the repair needs no destruction hook: a retained closure keeps the predecessor's bundle and core's own `capture-frame` fence refuses it, while the successor's first render receives a row pinned to the successor and routes. Safety of retained callbacks and liveness of fresh ones are one fact seen twice, and the routing suite now asserts both halves together in the cold, warm and explicitly-reset cache postures — the **positive contract** that replaced the failing-when-fixed recording — with the old late-binding mechanism rebuilt as a shipped negative control that reproduces both original failures. Second, a question for the owner, **since settled by ruling** (rf2-2l17): [React 3](lanes/design-laws.md#react-and-ownership) requires a render/commit tear to be *corrected before visible paint*, and the correction measured here was deferred to a macrotask, which carries no such ordering guarantee. The ruling is that the law stands as written — a hard pre-paint guarantee — and that the runtime is repaired rather than the law qualified, because the window is value-visible: the committed fiber holds the predecessor's values until the deferred `mark-dirty!` fires, and on an account or tenant switch those are another account's. Two things were wrong and both are fixed in `impl/collector.cljs`. The deferred phase now runs at the **microtask checkpoint**, which the event loop drains in full — including microtasks queued while draining, so React's own sync-lane flush joins it — before the same task's rendering update; a later task gets no such promise. And the repair's re-stamp is now **floored at one above the stamp it replaces**, because a same-id reincarnation restarts the frame term, so the basis alone could land on the number the cell already carried and did: measured in Chromium at epoch 3 → 3, with the notification delivered and ignored and the predecessor's value left on screen. The witness is `implementation/hicasso/test/re_frame/hicasso/reincarnation_paint_dom_cljs_test.cljs` — a boundary held mounted across a synchronous A→B switch, whose first rendering opportunity observes B, with restoring the `setTimeout` as its sabotage control and a companion row taking the same transition through the staged render→commit gap.

### The HMR contract (rf2-hic-015)

**The rule, and it is the un-generous one.** A reload re-mints every boundary a reloaded namespace defines. `defview` expands to `(def sym (mint-view! "<ns>/<sym>" body))`, and `mint-view!` allocates a fresh component and a fresh memo wrapper on every call, so the element type at that position changes — **unconditionally, including for a view whose own source did not change**, because it is the module's re-evaluation and not the edit that re-mints it. React replaces the subtree outright. **The default expectation across a save is therefore a clean remount and never preservation**, and a view's name is an address rather than an identity, exactly as a frame's public keyword is under [rf2-hic-013](#the-callback-and-frame-incarnation-rule-rf2-hic-013)'s rule. The memo comparator is not a rescue and cannot be: React consults `areEqual` only when the type already matches.

**What is lost.** Everything React keeps on the fiber at and below the re-minted boundary — component identity, hook state, refs and the instances they hold, the DOM nodes themselves. In a developer's terms: focus, selection and in-flight composition in a controlled field, a child's `useState` inside a host, an active imperative host's live instance, and any uncontrolled value the DOM was holding. Cleanups run and the subtree is built again from nothing. This is React's own consequence of a changed element type, which [I6](#1-invariants) assigns to React; the runtime adds nothing here and rescues nothing, and no preservation machinery was built for it.

**What survives.** Application state, because it is not a React fact: app-db, the registered handlers, and the frame. Re-running `(rf/make-frame {:id …})` — the ordinary shape of a re-initialising `^:dev/after-load` hook — is idempotent replacement, and it is measured as such: the incarnation token is the same object afterwards, the seeded db is intact, and the arm's frame-op memo is untouched, so a bundle captured before the save still addresses the same incarnation. A reload hook that instead **destroys** the frame before rebuilding it leaves this contract for [rf2-hic-013](#the-callback-and-frame-incarnation-rule-rf2-hic-013)'s, where a same-id successor seats and everything captured before the save belongs to the predecessor for the rest of its life. The defect that freeze recorded — a delayed operation resolving its target when it fires rather than when it was minted — has since been repaired under rf2-x874, so such an operation now refuses with `:rf.error/frame-destroyed` rather than silently writing the successor; what it does not do is survive the reload. Which of the two reload shapes a hook has is therefore still worth knowing before writing one: idempotent replacement keeps every pre-save bundle addressing the live incarnation, while destroy-then-rebuild retires all of them at once.

**The stale generation lets go completely.** Measured at the commit seam rather than in markup: after a save the key's cell carries exactly one reader and it is the *successor's* registration by identity; three successive saves still leave one; releasing the live generation returns cells, reader memberships, edges and the shared read-set entry to zero; and the hand-over does not depend on the order React commits the deletion and the insertion in. A count alone would not have settled it — a runtime that leaked the predecessor *and* failed to acquire for the successor also counts one — so the identity is asserted, and the bead's sabotage (withhold the predecessor's cleanup) is run as a real perturbation with its red and its restored green both asserted.

**Two windows a rendered assertion cannot see, and this is where the narrowing bites.** First, a save's re-registrations advance `registry-epoch`, a term of `commit-basis` that `make-snapshot` reads **only for a key no cell holds** — so one registration gets two correct answers: a mounted boundary's number ties, and an unrelated namespace's save costs the page nothing, while a boundary inside the render→commit gap sees the number move and its tear is corrected. Second, editing a `reg-sub` and saving drops the held reaction *synchronously* and rebuilds it **at the microtask checkpoint**; at the instant of the save the number React re-reads has not moved and no notification has fired, while a body re-run inside that window already answers with the edited subscription. Rendered markup is thus a green instrument on both sides of the window, and only the snapshot number, the notification count and the cell's reaction reference separate *repaired* from *never disturbed*. It is the same two-phase repair [rf2-hic-013](#the-callback-and-frame-incarnation-rule-rf2-hic-013) measured from the reincarnation side, reached here by the registry route, so it inherits that freeze's settlement along with its shape. When this was first measured the rebuild was a `setTimeout 0`, which hands the correction to a later task and so carries no ordering guarantee against paint, and [React 3](lanes/design-laws.md#react-and-ownership) requires one; but the deferral is one the runtime schedules in its own collector, so the ruling repaired the scheduling rather than qualifying the law. Rebuild, re-stamp and notification now land at the microtask checkpoint, inside the task that saved and ahead of its rendering update. Settled there, and therefore settled here.

**Every row is now measured, and the reload the browser rows run is a real one.** The paragraph above on what is lost was, until rf2-vsgq, React's documented consequence of a changed element type rather than this project's own measurement; it is now the latter. `implementation/scripts/serve-and-run-hicasso-hmr-testbed.cjs` starts `shadow-cljs watch`, rewrites one marked line of a real source file, and lets shadow recompile that namespace, push the module to a live page, re-evaluate it and run the app's `^:dev/after-load` hook — so what the witnesses see is the actual pipeline and not a model of it. Each save is confirmed by the NEW source literal appearing on screen, which is what separates *shadow carried code* from *a hook fired*. Thirty-six such reloads run per gate, and every row holds identically in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit.

**What the browser rows add to the Node ones.** A focused controlled field loses its node, its focus and its caret across a save while its VALUE stays byte-identical — the value is repainted from app-db, so a value assertion is green either way and only node identity, `activeElement` and the caret can see the remount. An in-flight composition on a *refusing* field is destroyed rather than stranded: the field afterwards shows the committed value, which it could not do if the draft had merely been re-accepted. A child's `useState` inside a host crossing comes back at zero **on a new fiber** — the counter and a per-mount instance id both move, which is what separates *the state was cleared* from *the component was rebuilt*. An active imperative host loses its instance and, with it, a `textContent` write React never learned about and no re-render can restore. Frame routing survives whole: two frames in two roots each keep painting their own state, a write after the reload still reaches only its own frame, and both incarnation tokens and both frame-op rows are the same objects afterwards — with a frame deliberately destroyed and rebuilt as the control that proves those readers can report a change. The hand-over leaves zero stale registrations, asserted by identity against the pre-save registrations and guarded by its own premise, so a save that replaced nothing cannot report a perfect hand-over.

**The sabotage, run at the renderer.** The audit asked for the lost-cleanup perturbation to turn red in a browser, and it does. The fault it names is a *renderer* fault, so it is injected at React's own seam — the unsubscribe React calls is swallowed — leaving the runtime unmodified. Armed, it reds every key at once: two registrations where there was one, the predecessor still present by identity, the retained census grown, and the retired generation still receiving notifications, which is the leak being live rather than a dead slot. Disarmed, the count HOLDS at two across two further saves rather than returning to one, and that is the honest shape of the restored green: a swallowed cleanup is unrecoverable, so the evidence that the hand-over works again is that it costs nothing further.

**What is still out of reach, named rather than implied.** A real IME composition RANGE stays Chromium-only (`Input.imeSetComposition` is a CDP method) and remains `freehand/test/re_frame/bench/hicasso/ime_run.cjs`'s; the browser rows drive the composition event sequence, which reaches the carve-out and the shadow but not the range. Selection as a RANGE across a save is not a question a remount leaves open — the node is gone — and range conduct across an out-of-band write is rf2-hic-016's. The two-phase repair a `reg-sub` edit produces is measured at the snapshot number and the notification count, neither of which is a browser fact, and stays with the registry suite. The witnesses are `implementation/hicasso/test/re_frame/hicasso/hmr_remount_cljs_test.cljs` and its `hmr_registry_cljs_test.cljs` companion for the commit seam, and `implementation/hicasso/testbed/hmr_spec.cjs` for the browser.

### The ambient-read extent as enforced (rf2-hic-011)

**The rule, and the reason it is enforceable rather than aspirational.** `sub` is legal exactly during the direct synchronous execution of the active boundary body, helpers, branches and loops included, however deep and however dynamic; every crossing out of that extent refuses. The extent is a **dynamic** one and not a lexical one, and that is what makes it checkable: an ordinary helper two call frames down is inside it, and a closure written on the body's own line is outside it the instant something calls it later. Nothing about the shape or the position of the code decides. The only question is whether a boundary body is on the stack at the moment the read happens, and the runtime answers it by reading one slot that `run-once` sets in a `try` and clears in the matching `finally`.

**The legality matrix, as measured.** Every row is a test in `implementation/hicasso/test/re_frame/hicasso/read_extent_cljs_test.cljs`.

| Crossing | Legal | What decides it |
|---|---|---|
| A nested helper, at any depth | ✓ | Its reads are the enclosing boundary's, because the extent is the body's dynamic extent and a helper has no read set of its own to own them |
| A branch | ✓ | The arm that ran contributes its keys and the arm that did not contributes none — the edge is recorded where the read happens |
| A variable-length loop | ✓ | The collector window closes around the codec, which is eager, so a `for` is driven to exhaustion inside the window that wrote it |
| A render prop | ✗ | It is invoked during a render, but not during *this* body's render, and the runtime will not guess which render owns the read |
| An event callback | ✗ | It fires after the extent unwound. Its *dispatch* still works — the ambient dispatch is captured at lowering time on purpose — so the fence is on reads and not on callbacks |
| A promise continuation | ✗ | The microtask runs after the body returned |
| A timer callback | ✗ | Same, one macrotask later |
| A lazy sequence that escaped the render | ✗ | Realised with no body on the stack. The same carrier inside the window is the legal loop row above, which is the whole point of the pair |
| A module-load read | ✗ | A top-level form evaluates with no body, no frame and no root |
| An unforced `delay` at a boundary crossing | ✗ | Refused by the crossing walk rather than by the frame slot — see below |
| A deferral parked in a mutable reference and forced inside another body | **not refused** | The declared limit, below |

Each `✗` carries a stable id, the emitting symbol, an actionable recovery keyword and the offending query, and [rf2-hic-007](specification.md#36-loud-stable-failure)'s error-shape constructor has since landed and settled what else it can carry. The settlement is narrower than this paragraph originally promised, and deliberately so. The constructor supplies `:view` and `:source` from the ambient declaration extent — but `:rf.error/hicasso-sub-outside-render` is precisely the refusal raised when NO boundary is rendering, so for these rows both are ABSENT, and that absence is the correct report rather than a missing feature: a nil `:view` would claim the read happened inside a boundary the runtime could not name. The frame, the tree path or host-prop position and the expected shape are likewise absent here, and for the same kind of reason — the refusal's whole subject is that there is no render extent, so there is no frame to name and no position to point at. Those three are per-CLASS slots, enumerated per id in the Hicasso section of `spec/009-Instrumentation.md`, not fields the constructor can add to every refusal. The `:where` symbol still stands in for the file, line and column at these rows, and now does so by construction rather than pending anything. The six frame-slot rows refuse with `:rf.error/hicasso-sub-outside-render` and recover to `:read-inside-a-boundary-body`, and the message names `subscribe-once` as the sanctioned snapshot for handler and utility code. The tests assert the id, the `:where`, the recovery and the query as an exact map and assert only that the *prose* names the recovery route — freezing the wording would collide with that constructor and rf2-hic-021's id catalogue, neither of which has landed.

**Two enforcers, not one, and the matrix needs both.** The frame slot cannot see a deferral forced inside *another* body's render, because a body genuinely is running there — the wrong one. `re-frame.hicasso.impl.codec/realize-deep` closes the structural half at the boundary crossing: it forces every lazy sequence reachable from a boundary's props inside the window of the body that *wrote* it, and it **refuses** an unforced `delay` with `:rf.error/hicasso-deferred-read-at-boundary` rather than forcing it, because forcing an author's explicit deferral would change what their program means. A `delay` the author already deref'd in their own body carries a computed value and crosses freely, which is the control that keeps the refusal from being "the walk refuses every delay".

**The residue neither enforcer reaches, measured rather than disclaimed.** `realize-deep`'s reach is the walk's reach: it descends into data structures, and a mutable reference is not one. A thunk an author parks in an atom is invisible to the structural pass, and if something forces it inside a different body's render the frame slot finds a frame and is satisfied — so the read is filed under a boundary that never wrote it, the page paints correctly, and the edge is wrong. The suite measures both halves of that: forced outside a render it refuses like every other escape, forced inside a foreign body it silently succeeds and the misattribution is asserted as the read set of the wrong boundary. If the hole is ever closed, that row is the one that goes red, which is what it is for.

**The reconciliation clause, and the number that carries it.** I7's third sentence says dynamic reads reconcile from the selected render without a general per-boundary dependency ledger, and there is indeed no ledger: a changed read set is a different cached entry, a different `subscribe`, and therefore a fresh registration whose held edge set is empty by construction, so the replacement *is* React's own cleanup plus its own re-subscribe. What is asserted is the outcome, per key — the key that stayed keeps exactly **one** reader, the key that disappeared keeps none, the key that appeared gains one — and the surviving reader is checked **by identity** to be the successor's. The two assertions catch different faults and neither subsumes the other. Acquiring for the successor without releasing the predecessor leaves **two** reader-array entries on the key that stayed, and the count catches that on its own. What the identity check adds is the forward edge: that the one remaining entry is the successor's registration and not the predecessor's, kept stale while the count still reads one.

**Every refusal was shown to fail first, and one perturbation changed the design of the suite.** Four perturbations were applied to the runtime, run, and reverted; none is committed. Removing the nil-frame guard from `read-key!` reds seven rows — the six frame-slot refusals and the declared-limit row — and it does **not** stop the escaped reads from throwing: they fall through to `subscribe-once`, which raises `:rf.error/no-frame-context` instead. A witness written as "something threw" would therefore have stayed **green** under the exact sabotage this freeze exists to catch, which is why every row asserts the whole ex-data map rather than the fact of a throw. Removing the unconditional scratch reset from `run-once` reds five rows, and it initially failed to red one of them: the row's follow-up body happened to re-read a key the previous body had also read, so the accumulated key was swallowed by its own expected set. The row now reads keys disjoint from the render that deferred into it. Removing the crossing refusal from `realize-deep` reds the `delay` row alone. Removing `read-key!`'s push onto the scratch buffer is the widest of the four at nine rows, because the scratch is how a legal read comes to be recorded at all: the three legality rows and the reconciliation row go red alongside three of the refusals, the `delay` row and the declared limit.

**What is not measured, and is owed.** Two deferral carriers have no witness here and are owed to a browser suite: a read deferred into a **React effect**, and a read deferred across a **Suspense retry or an Activity reveal**. Both are the same discriminator and neither is asserted, so this paragraph says so rather than letting the matrix imply completeness. The event-callback and render-prop rows *are* witnessed, at the exact lowered wrapper React would call, taken off the element the render emitted; what a browser would add there is the DOM's own dispatch and not the law.

### The two-hook ceiling, and the chosen collector substrate (rf2-hic-018)

**The ceiling, frozen.** An ordinary boundary's shell calls exactly two React hooks — `useContext(frame-context)` for the frame and `useSyncExternalStore(subscribe, getSnapshot)` for the subscription epoch — and **the count does not move with the read count**. There is no third hook and no per-instance render-phase state at all: no `useRef`, no `useState`, no hook cell held for storage. That absence is what makes the budget reachable rather than merely declared, because React gives a function component no per-instance storage except a hook cell, so a shell that wants one has spent its third hook before it starts. Both closures are instead pure functions of a value and live on a read-set entry shared by every boundary reading the same set.

**The measurement, and why it is one.** `implementation/hicasso/test/re_frame/hicasso/hook_budget_cljs_test.cljs` (rf2-wjag) counts the calls **React's own dispatcher received**, through a probe wrapping the dispatcher slot; the runtime's `shell-hook-ledger` is a declaration and is what the counted calls are checked against, never the evidence. Because every assertion has the form *exactly these two*, and a prohibition is trivially satisfied by an instrument that cannot report more, the suite's first row drives a control component calling `useRef`, `useState` and `useMemo` and requires the probe to answer all three by name and in call order. The budget is then counted per boundary — a nested boundary costs its own two and shares nothing that would make a page's total sublinear — and counted at one, seven and twenty reads, where it reads two every time. That last row is the whole argument for the ambient collector: *N* reads = *N* hooks satisfies this budget on no rung. A `defhost` crossing costs the door one hook under a gated server policy and none under the render policy, and the shell's ledger does not move either way. The witness runs a real server render in the node lane, which is in the fast-PR spine, because a budget breach is a fact about the shell's source rather than about the host.

**It is a ceiling, not a target.** `collector/frame-prop-shell` declares one hook and carries its own ledger; it is a hypothesis under measurement rather than the default, and I9's own wording already prices taking it up — *freeing or replacing one requires its own correctness and whole-shell measurement*. What the ceiling forbids is the operative half: no optional capability may add a hook to **every** boundary, and the [React-compatibility lane](lanes/react-compatibility-notes.md) already refuses one concrete facility on exactly that ground.

**The substrate, chosen.** The collector reaches its reactions through one door — `subs/subscribe`, routed to the installed adapter's `make-derived-value` — and the reference has two materially different implementations of that function: the ratom family's `reagent.ratom/make-reaction` and the React-hook spine's derived-value container in core. Until now Hicasso installed neither, so the substrate under the collector was whichever adapter the application chose, and the same collector measures **1,417 or 2,115 B per read** on the package depending only on that. **Hicasso installs its own adapter, and it carries the React-hook spine** — the substrate every kernel witness in this package has ever run on, shipping in the core dependency the package already declares, at a carried premium of **+698 B/read** against the ratom family. The evidence, the axes on which it is one-sided, the reopen conditions, and the disposition of the read-free shell breach that follows from it are in [the substrate decision record](substrate-decision.md).

**What the substrate choice does not do is move the shell.** The R=0 boundary reads `1,100 B` and `1,095 B` on the two substrates — five bytes apart, because the shell touches no adapter — against the frozen `1,024 B` line, with all twelve package readings at or above `1,087 B`. Remediation through the substrate is therefore refused on the evidence rather than deferred, the breach is carried red and unnormalised, and the line is not re-registered here; the three routes that remain are priced in [the record](substrate-decision.md#52-the-read-free-boundary-shell--the-disposition).
